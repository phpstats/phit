Probit: a normal latent variable and Fisher scoring

1. Meaningful simulated observations

We observe simulated students, with study hours, a practice score and a binary pass/fail outcome. Inputs are observed data, not guesses. The column of ones is constructed to estimate an intercept. R seed 20261013 generates 120 students: hours are uniform between 1 and 12, scores are clipped to 20–95 from 45 + 3 × hours + normal noise with standard deviation 8. Both are rounded to two decimals.

The underlying simulation is a latent normal threshold model:

\[z_i^*=-1.9+0.13\,\mathrm{hours}_i+0.011\,\mathrm{score}_i+\epsilon_i, \quad \epsilon_i\sim N(0,1),\quad y_i=\mathbf{1}\{z_i^*>0\}.\]

With \(\eta_i=x_i^\top\beta\), symmetry of the standard normal gives \(P(y_i=1)=P(\epsilon_i>-\eta_i)=\Phi(\eta_i)\). The latent value is a model construction: only the resulting 0 or 1 is supplied to the estimator. Fixing the latent variance to one identifies the coefficient scale; there is no sigma to estimate.

The normal CDF \(\Phi(a)\) is the area under a standard-normal density to the left of \(a\). The density is \(\phi(a)=e^{-a^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\), and \(\Phi'(a)=\phi(a)\). Unlike a logit coefficient, \(e^{\beta_j}\) is not a constant odds multiplier. The local probability slope is \(\phi(\eta_i)\beta_j\), so it varies by observation.

2. Likelihood and derivatives

For independent Bernoulli outcomes the log-likelihood is

\[\ell(\beta)=\sum_i[y_i\log\Phi(\eta_i)+(1-y_i)\log\Phi(-\eta_i)].\]

The complement is written \(\Phi(-\eta)\) to avoid subtracting a CDF rounded to one. Taking a derivative with respect to \(\eta_i\) gives

\[s_i=\phi(\eta_i)\frac{y_i-\pi_i}{\pi_i(1-\pi_i)},\qquad \pi_i=\Phi(\eta_i),\qquad g=X^\top s.\]

For a success this is \(\phi/\Phi(\eta)\); for a failure it is \(-\phi/\Phi(-\eta)\). These ratios are evaluated from log density minus log tail probability. Rust uses erfc for ordinary tails and a normal Mills-ratio continued fraction beyond -8, preserving finite log-likelihoods even when the CDF underflows.

Expected negative curvature, or Fisher information, is

\[I=X^\top W X,\qquad w_i=\frac{\phi(\eta_i)^2}{\pi_i(1-\pi_i)}.\]

One way to see this is to square \(s_i\) and take its expectation: \(E[(y_i-\pi_i)^2]=\pi_i(1-\pi_i)\), leaving the expression above. Unlike canonical logistic regression, observed curvature and expected information need not agree at a finite probit sample. We use Fisher scoring and its covariance, matching R glm and statsmodels GLM; a discrete-model probit fit’s observed-Hessian covariance can differ without either calculation being erroneous.

3. Scoring, weighted matrices and SVD

Fisher scoring solves \(I\Delta\beta=g\) and updates coefficients. For Poisson this is also the Newton step, since information equals negative Hessian. To avoid forming and inverting the cross-product for fitting, define column scales \(D\), \(A=XD^{-1}\) and \(\theta=D\beta\). Solve

\[B\Delta\theta\approx q,\quad B=W^{1/2}A,\quad q=W^{-1/2}s, \qquad B=USV^\top,\quad \Delta\theta=VS^{-1}U^\top q.\]

The weighted least-squares normal equations are \(A^\top W A\Delta\theta=A^\top s\). This is the same update in scaled coordinates, with \(\Delta\beta=D^{-1}\Delta\theta\). We display cross-products to understand them; the production solve uses weighted SVD. See the OLS SVD explanation.

Weights and scores change each iteration: this is IRLS. The full increment can overshoot, so a decreasing-likelihood step is halved, at most 30 times. Both a small full coefficient increment and a small likelihood change are required. A tiny damped step alone does not establish convergence; the limit is 100 updates.

def values(eta):
    log_density = -0.5*eta**2-0.5*np.log(2*np.pi)
    probability = ndtr(eta)
    root = np.exp(log_density-0.5*(log_ndtr(eta)+log_ndtr(-eta)))
    score = np.where(y == 1, np.exp(log_density-log_ndtr(eta)), -np.exp(log_density-log_ndtr(-eta)))
    return probability,root,score

def log_likelihood(eta):
    return np.where(y == 1,log_ndtr(eta),log_ndtr(-eta)).sum()

scales = np.linalg.norm(X,axis=0)

4. Covariance and Wald inference

Evaluate information at the final coefficients, then compute

\[\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta)=I(\hat\beta)^{-1},\quad \operatorname{SE}_j=\sqrt{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\beta)_{jj}},\quad z_j=\hat\beta_j/\operatorname{SE}_j.\]

The SVD supplies the covariance as \(D^{-1}VS^{-2}V^\top D^{-1}\). Two-sided p-values use \(2P(Z\ge |z_j|)\) and 95% Wald limits use \(\hat\beta_j\pm1.959964\operatorname{SE}_j\). These are asymptotic normal tests, not OLS Student t tests. We do not multiply covariance by RSS/(n-p). R confint.default() supplies equivalent Wald intervals; ordinary GLM confint() uses profile likelihood instead.

_,singular,Vt=np.linalg.svd(root[:,None]*A,full_matrices=False)
covariance=((Vt.T/singular)@(Vt/singular[:,None]))/scales[:,None]/scales[None,:]
se=np.sqrt(np.diag(covariance))
z=beta/se
p_values=2*norm.sf(np.abs(z))
conf_int=np.column_stack([beta-norm.ppf(.975)*se,beta+norm.ppf(.975)*se])

5. Likelihood, deviance and AIC

For individual binary outcomes the saturated likelihood is zero, giving \(D=-2\ell(\hat\beta)\). The intercept-only null probability is the observed success proportion. Without a ones column, the null baseline is 0.5. Neither a latent variance nor an OLS residual variance is estimated. A probit coefficient is on the latent normal scale: multiplying it by a normal density converts it to a local probability slope, not an odds ratio.

AIC is \(2p-2\ell(\hat\beta)\); residual degrees of freedom are \(n-p\), without an estimated dispersion parameter. Predictions are probabilities, and residuals are observed minus fitted. These vectors follow retained input row order.

ll=log_likelihood(eta)
null_ll=np.sum(y*np.log(y.mean())+(1-y)*np.log1p(-y.mean()))
model_deviance=-2*ll
null_deviance=-2*null_ll

6. Assumptions, failure modes and experiments

Both observed classes must remain after missing-row deletion. Complete or quasi separation can make a finite MLE nonexistent; encountered separating directions are rejected, as are singular information and iteration exhaustion. This certificate check is not exhaustive separation detection. Near separation can produce poor Wald inference even for a converged fit. Try replacing passed by study_hours > 6: what would happen to the latent coefficient as the probabilities approach the labels?

For both methods the retained design must have n > p and full numerical rank. Missing null/NAN rows are dropped with a warning unless missing: ‘raise’. No partial unconverged result is returned. See the contract and the executed three-way comparison.

Rust connections

fit_probit owns its input vectors and converts them to nalgebra matrices. Borrowed matrix operands remain available for later iterations; iterators construct weights and score residuals. A dedicated loop performs this estimator’s updates. Result and ? propagate validation/numerical failures. The returned struct holds numeric fields; the PHP binding converts those fields into an associative array and adds warning/sample metadata. No generic optimizer or family hierarchy is added. See Rust learning notes and crates/stats-core/src/probit.rs.
